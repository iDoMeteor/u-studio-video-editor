## U Stu Video Editor
Welcome to U Stu Video Editor, a video editor made for the GNOME desktop. In this tour, we'll build a short edit from start to finish.

## Import
Let's start by importing some clips. Pick several files at once, and they land in order on the timeline. The project takes its size and frame rate from the first clip.

## Clip tooltips
Rest the pointer on any clip to see the frame under it, its timecode, and its details.

## Mixed frame rates
These clips are a different size, and run at twenty-five frames a second instead of thirty. That's fine: they join the project, and every frame lands at the right time.

## Import a folder
Import Folder brings in a whole folder at once. It's a single undo step, and anything that can't be opened is listed for you.

## Media browser
The media browser shows everything in your project, with its length, frame rate and format. Drag any item straight onto the timeline.

## Split audio
Here's the finished soundtrack for this video. Split Audio peels its sound onto an audio track of its own, so we can work with it separately.

## Trim to fit
Drag the end of the soundtrack back, and it snaps neatly to the end of the edit.

## Playback
Press play. Playback runs in real time, with sound, and the editor stays responsive the whole time.

## Shuttle and step
J, K and L shuttle backwards and forwards; press again to go faster. The arrow keys step one frame at a time, or ten at once.

## Loop a range
Set a loop in and out at the playhead, and playback goes round and round that range. Handy for fine-tuning a moment.

## Edit on the timeline
Now for some editing. We'll work on the unicorn clips on the bottom track, with an empty track on top.

## Move
Drag a clip anywhere you like, even onto another track.

## Trim
Drag a clip's edge to trim it. The gap it leaves is yours to fill.

## Ripple trim (Alt+drag)
Hold Alt while you trim, and the clips after it follow the edge, so no gap opens up.

## Slip (Shift+drag)
Hold Shift and drag to slip a clip: it keeps its place and length, but shows a different part of the source.

## Dissolve
Right-click a cut and choose Add Transition, for a smooth dissolve from one clip to the next.

## Copy (Ctrl+drag)
Hold Control while you drag, and you drop a copy instead of moving the clip.

## Nudge
Comma and period nudge the selection by a single frame. Add Shift to move ten frames at a time.

## Split and ripple delete
X splits the clip at the playhead. Shift and Delete removes a piece and closes the gap behind it.

## Markers
Press M to drop a marker at the playhead, so you can find that moment again later.

## Rubber band select (Shift+drag)
Hold Shift and drag across the timeline, and every clip the box touches is selected.

## Ripple mode
Press R for ripple mode. Now moving a clip closes the gap it leaves, and pushes later clips along.

## Undo and redo
Every edit is a single undo step. Control Z takes it back, and Shift Control Z brings it back again.

## Save
An untitled project asks where to save it. After that, Save writes straight to the same file.

## Automatic backups
And every save keeps the previous version as a backup next to your project, so a mistake is never final.

## Save As
Right-click the Save button when you want a copy under a new name.

## Settings
Settings has every preference in one place, grouped into pages.

## Settings: Toggles
Toggles switches everyday behaviours on and off: reopening your last project, snapping, following the playhead, thumbnails and waveforms.

## Settings: Performance
Performance is where you tune the preview, proxies, graphics acceleration and background work.

## Settings: Locations
Locations sets where your projects are saved, and where finished videos go.

## Settings: Render
Render holds your render profiles. High quality is the default.

## Settings: Drop-ins
Drop-ins lists optional add-ons, like titles, and lets you turn each one on or off.

## Settings: Shortcuts
And Shortcuts lists every action with its keyboard shortcut.

## Project format
Click the format under the project name to change the frame rate or the background colour. Every clip, marker and dissolve keeps its timing.

## Render
Time to render. The file is named for you and saved in your export folder, in high quality by default.

## Queue another render
Right-click the Render button to queue another version, like a quick draft.

## Cancel or queue
Clicking the Render button while it works lets you cancel the current render, and the next one in the queue carries on.

## Render finished
When the render is done, the button turns cyan. Click it to open your new video.

## Quit while rendering
If you quit in the middle of a render, the editor asks first, stops cleanly, and offers to finish the job next time.

## Reopen
Open the editor again, and your last project is right where you left it, with the unfinished render ready to restart.

## Help
Help explains every control and lists every shortcut, in sections that remember where you were.

## Release notes
Release notes show what's new in each version.

## About and diagnostics
And About has the version details. Copy Diagnostics puts everything a bug report needs on your clipboard.

## U Stu Video Editor #2
That's U Stu Video Editor. Thanks for watching!
